Disaster Response Decision Engine

A decision-support engine for earthquake + urban flash-flood response, built around one hard constraint: all comms infrastructure is destroyed. Drones are the only sensors; the engine never sees ground truth, only noisy detections it has to fuse itself.

How we got here (the honest version)

Our first evaluation showed the engine losing to a much simpler baseline. An "informed operator" (real belief fusion + correct resource-type matching, but zero survival scoring, cascade bonus, or connectivity-aware routing) out-rescued the full engine on average, 56.9 vs 49.0 people. That's a real result from a real run, not staged.
We diagnosed it instead of tuning around it. Instrumenting every dispatched mission showed >97% were failing, mostly a fixed 50m search radius missing victims whose belief position was routinely 40-60m off, and wrong-type dispatches (a boat sent to a buried victim) that could repeat forever with nothing learned. At that failure rate, which target you pick barely matters, raw dispatch volume dominates the outcome, which is exactly why the simpler, faster-dispatching baseline was winning.
Two realism fixes, justified as "what real responders do," not as tuning: search radius now scales with the belief's own reported uncertainty (and costs real time proportional to the area searched, so a vague fix costs more than a precise one); and a wrong-type failure now generates a field report (the crew radios back what they actually found), correcting the belief instead of letting the same wrong dispatch repeat.
With missions actually succeeding, the picture changed. Per-mission success rate rose 5.6x for the full engine (1.6% → 8.9%) and 6.4x for the informed-operator baseline. The engine/baseline gap, which was real and sizeable before, closed to statistical noise. Two of the engine's three scoring features started showing a measurable, correctly-directioned effect for the first time; re-run at 100 seeds specifically to check whether that held up under more data. It didn't, for one of them, see below.

Cascade bonus: reported as a null result, not spun. At 30 seeds it looked promising (paired t = +1.60, favourable on 19/30 seeds). We didn't stop there: we re-ran at 100 seeds specifically to test whether that would hold up, and it didn't, t dropped to +0.26 (53/44 split, essentially a coin flip). The 30-seed signal was sampling noise, not a real effect. This project's headline novel idea, after two independent fixes to the mechanism it depends on, still shows no measurable aggregate benefit.
Rescuer risk: borderline, not proven. Moved from t=+1.54 (30 seeds) to t=+1.92 (100 seeds), right at the edge of conventional significance (p≈0.057, just short of the usual 1.96 threshold), with 47 of 100 seeds tied exactly (the penalty often doesn't change which candidate wins at all). Suggestive, not conclusive.
Silence-as-signal: still no effect. t=-1.14 at 100 seeds, if anything trending slightly negative. No positive contribution found.
